## Current Update

{date: updated}

The Budget Lab's labor market analysis, updated to incorporate May 2026 CPS microdata, does not provide clear evidence of labor market disruption associated with AI. Churn across occupations, AI exposure among the unemployed, and comparisons of AI-exposed and unexposed workers all remain flat, lie within historical ranges, or continue along pre-AI trends. 

Of course, these findings may change as new data become available. The Budget Lab plans to continue monitoring AI-relevant trends to assess how job impacts might change.


## Introduction

This interactive tool contains The Budget Lab's ongoing analysis of potential AI impacts on the labor market. Major categories of analysis can be accessed by clicking tabs towards the top of the page with the following names: Occupational churn, AI metrics, and Comparisons of AI-exposed and unexposed workers. After selecting one of those tabs, users should note the options listed on the left-hand sidebar. Those options generate different views of the data: for example, occupational churn (i.e., dissimilarity) can be tracked for the entire labor force or for specific industries. 

Detailed descriptions of the analysis can be found in the Budget Lab documents that introduce each. Occupational churn, AI-exposure rates of the unemployed, and related analysis can be found in <a href="https://budgetlab.yale.edu/research/evaluating-impact-ai-labor-market-current-state-affairs">Gimbel et al. (2025)</a>. Details of AI exposure metrics are discussed in <a href="https://budgetlab.yale.edu/research/labor-market-ai-exposure-what-do-we-know">Gimbel, Kendall, and Kulsakdinun (2026)</a>. The synthetic differences-in-differences analysis of AI-exposed and unexposed workers is introduced in <a href="https://budgetlab.yale.edu/research/what-we-do-and-dont-know-about-how-ai-affecting-labor-market">Gimbel, Kendall, and Nunn (2026)</a>. 

## AI-Exposure Metrics and Crosswalks

The Budget Lab makes use of a collection of AI-exposure metrics which must be mapped to occupation codes. The crosswalked data are available in the data download linked in the sidebar. More detail about their construction can be found in <a href="https://budgetlab.yale.edu/research/labor-market-ai-exposure-what-do-we-know">Gimbel, Kendall, and Kulsakdinun (2026)</a>. Exposure metrics are made available by <a href="https://github.com/AIOE-Data/AIOE">Felten, Raj, and Seamans (2021)</a>, <a href="https://github.com/openai/GPTs-are-GPTs">Eloundou et al. (2023)</a>, <a href="https://artificialminushuman.com/">Eisfeldt et al. (2023)</a>, and <a href="https://github.com/microsoft/working-with-ai">Tomlinson et al. (2025)</a>. Usage data are made available by <a href="https://www.anthropic.com/research/economic-index-march-2026-report">Massenkoff et al. (2026)</a>. Occupation-level data are available at both the SOC and CPS code levels.
