## The State of Tariffs

This tool presents the Budget Lab's latest analysis of tariff policy. Introduced in July of 2026,
this webpage consolidates several of our tariff-related workstreams that were previously organized
as separate reports. The tool has five tabs:

- **Introduction.** This overview, along with a summary of what has changed since our last update.
- **Daily Statutory Rates.** Our estimates of weighted-average statutory tariff rates (sometimes
  called the "applied" tariff rates) calculated for each day starting in January 2025, both overall
  and broken out by legal authority, trading partner, and product.
- **Projected Effects: Default Scenario.** Our estimates of the economic and fiscal effects of
  tariff policy as specified by current law.
- **Projected Effects: Alternative Scenarios.** The same estimated effects, compared across
  different assumptions for how tariff policy evolves going forward.
- **Methodology.** Technical details on how estimates are produced.

## Changes since the last update

Since our April 2026 release, we have made three methodological changes:

- **Fixed a model bug in rate increase measurement.** In the prior release, baseline MFN tariffs
  on some products were being counted as contributing to the above-baseline ETR increase that
  feeds into the price model. We have corrected this model bug.
- **Refined our measurement of statutory tariff rates.** The Tariff Rate Tracker now more closely
  aligns HTS revisions with the dates policies took effect and incorporates updated modeling of
  Section 232 tariffs, Section 301 exclusions, statutory exemption lists, USMCA preference use,
  and the pre-2025 MFN baseline.
- **Replaced a flat avoidance assumption with an adjustment calibrated to observed collections.**
  Our previous model reduced projected tariff revenue by a uniform amount to account for evasion.
  The updated model instead compares statutory rates with duties observed in Census trade data and
  estimates the gap separately by sector and trading-partner group. This adjustment captures not
  only evasion, but also exemptions, preference claims, tariff avoidance, and measurement error
  that are not fully visible in the statutory rate record. It is applied upstream throughout the
  model, including in estimates of prices, household costs, trade responses, and revenue.

Together, these changes lower our estimates of effective tariff rates, consumer-price effects, and
average household costs relative to our April release. Revenue changes less because the earlier
model already included an avoidance adjustment in that calculation. Because the new calibration is
based on experience under the 2025–2026 tariff regime, we will continue to update it as more data
become available.
