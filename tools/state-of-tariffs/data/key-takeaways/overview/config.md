## Key Takeaways

{date: updated}

- **Tariff rates are at their highest level in generations.** Under current law, the average
  statutory U.S. tariff rate reaches about 9% by the end of 2026. After accounting for
  noncompliance and shifts in trade, the effective rate is closer to 7%.
- **The cost shows up as higher consumer prices.** We estimate that tariff policy raises the
  consumer price level by roughly 0.3% in the medium run — about $400–$500 per household per year
  on average.
- **The burden is regressive.** Because lower-income households spend a larger share of their
  income on goods, tariffs take a bigger bite relative to income at the bottom of the distribution
  than at the top.
- **Tariffs raise sizable revenue but modestly shrink the economy.** We project on the order of
  $1.2 trillion in net new federal revenue over ten years, alongside a small long-run reduction in
  the level of real GDP.
- **The path forward is still a choice.** Our default scenario reflects current law, under which
  Section 122 tariffs expire at the end of July. The **Alternative Scenarios** tab shows how these
  effects change under other assumptions, including a new Section 301 regime.
